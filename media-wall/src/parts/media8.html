Media wall proposal

The main plan is now the artisan oak build: oak used as furniture and joinery against a wall painted in Little Greene Sage Green. Below the worktop is one full-width unit: bays 1 and 2 are IKEA BESTÅ 60×40×64 frames on a 9 cm plinth, bay 3 is a custom plywood carcass with two face-out record trays, and the 45 cm bay at the right stays open for the subwoofer, all behind one oak face frame under a 3 cm solid oak worktop at 76 cm. Above it, two solid oak uprights frame the TV and carry the shelves on joints left on show: wedged through-tenons, pegged housings and a record ledge above the screen. Each bay holds one family: Xbox under the Series X, PlayStation under the TV, vinyl under the turntable. Every console fits even at its largest version, every record and single is face-out, and every cable drops into a hidden channel.

Built to your alcove: 225 cm wide, 50 cm deep, floor to ceiling (220 cm)

  • Bays 1 and 2: two BESTÅ frames, each 60 × 40 × 64 cm, stand on a 9 cm plywood plinth, so their tops are at 73 cm. Back panels off. The oak face frame's bottom rail covers the plinth, so it never shows.
  • Bay 3: a custom carcass in 18 mm birch ply, 60 × 40 × 73 cm, standing on the floor with its top level with the frames. Inside it is 56.4 cm wide and 69.4 cm tall: exactly two face-out tiers of 34.7 cm. The drawer runners screw straight into the ply sides, so it needs no liners.
  • Fixing: the frames and carcass are bolted to each other, stand 10 cm off the back wall on spacer blocks, and are fixed to it with anti-tip brackets. With two trays of about 32 kg each, the anti-tip brackets on bay 3 are essential, and only one record tray should be open at a time.
  • Worktop: solid oak, full stave or wide board, 225 × 40 × 3 cm with a chamfered front edge, flush with the fronts, top at 76 cm. The 10 cm left open behind it is the slot for cables and rising warm air.
  • Oak uprights: two, 4.5 × 30 cm, from the worktop to the ceiling at 44.5–49 and 175.5–180 cm from the left wall, about 2 cm clear of the TV each side. They carry the high shelf, the record ledge, the left shelf and the turntable shelf (see "The oak joinery").
  • Power: everything runs from the wall socket low on the back wall behind bay 1, swapped from a single to a double: one socket for your 10-gang behind the TV, one for the 6-way at the back of the sub bay. Their leads run along the mains side of the channel.
  • Inside bays 1 and 2: about 56.8 cm wide, 38–40 cm deep with the back open, and 60.8 cm high, exactly as before: every row and console just sits 9 cm higher. The game drawer's base is now 11.6 cm off the floor, easier to reach.

Why the unit is 76 cm tall

Two face-out drawers stacked need more height than a BESTÅ frame has inside, and the worktop has to stay level across the alcove because the TV spans bays 1 to 3. So the whole unit goes up 9 cm, from 67 to about 76 cm.

The record trays

Bay 3, under the turntable, is two roll-out trays stacked, and everything in them is face-out. LPs stand in a trough on the left of each tray, leaning back about 10° like a record shop browser; 7-inch singles stand the same way in a narrower trough on the right, where the singles and spare sleeves used to stand spine-up. With a tray shut, the room sees the front LP and the front single above a slim oak lip. Pull it out and flip through both rows of covers from the front.

What changed, and why

  • Thinner trays: 9 mm birch ply sides, front and back instead of 12 mm, a 12 mm base instead of 18 mm, a 6 mm oak divider, and a slimmer oak lip, 5 cm tall and 15 mm thick. It looks lighter from the room and shows more of each front cover.
  • What the slimmer build buys: 4.2 cm more width inside each tray, enough to turn the singles to face-out (a 7-inch sleeve is 18.3 cm), and 6 mm more finger room above every row.
  • Spare inner sleeves move to the kit shelf or the hidden drawer.

How the system works

  • LP trough: 32.6 cm wide, so a bare 31.4 cm sleeve has 6 mm each side. Records lean back 10° against each other; the last one leans on a raked oak rest at the back.
  • Singles trough: 18.9 cm wide, the same lean, a smaller rest at the back.
  • Capacity: the inside is 38.2 cm deep. The lean takes about 5.5 cm for LPs, leaving about 31 cm of stack: about 90 LPs at about 3.3 mm each, allowing for gatefolds. Singles lean less far, leaving about 34 cm: about 120 at about 2.8 mm. Two trays: about 180 LPs and 240 singles.
  • Outer plastic sleeves: if most of your LPs live in thick outer sleeves (up to about 32.3 cm), widen the LP trough to 33.4 cm; the singles trough then drops to 18.1 cm, too narrow for face-out singles, so they go back to spine-up.
  • Runners: 400 mm full-extension ball-bearing runners rated 68–70 kg per pair (45 kg is the minimum), screwed straight into the carcass's 18 mm ply sides. The whole tray clears the unit, so the back records are as easy to reach as the front.
  • Weight and safety: about 32 kg per full tray. The carcass must be fixed to the wall with anti-tip brackets before the trays are loaded, and only one tray opened at a time.

The 12 mm base carries about 27 kg of records. Glue and screw it under the sides, front and back all round, and glue the oak divider down to it: the divider stiffens the middle, so the base doesn't sag over time.

How to build them

  1. Build the carcass: glue and screw the top and bottom between the sides, square, with the two back rails. Have the merchant cut the ply to the list.
  2. Set it in place beside bay 2 on a flat floor, shim it level with the frames' tops at 73, bolt it to bay 2's frame, and fix it to the wall with anti-tip brackets.
  3. Fit the runners straight onto the ply sides, level, every screw hole used: the lower pair so the tray's underside sits at 2.3 cm, the upper pair at 37.0 cm. Make the tray width exactly what the runner instructions specify (normally the opening less 25 mm).
  4. Build the trays: sides, back and low front panel glued and pinned to each other, then glued and screwed down onto the 12 mm base. Glue the oak divider 32.6 cm in from the left inside face, fit the two raked rests, and glue the felt down.
  5. Hang the trays and check they run true empty.
  6. Fit the oak lips from inside the tray, with a hand-cut finger pull at the top centre, 2–3 mm clear of the face frame.
  7. Load gradually, one tray at a time, and check both still run smoothly full.
