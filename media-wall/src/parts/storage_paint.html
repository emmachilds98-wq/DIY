If the collection grows

  • Overflow cube unit: a 1 × 4 or 2 × 4 cube unit such as IKEA KALLAX (cubes about 33 cm inside, 39 cm deep) holds about 60–70 LPs per cube. On its side behind the sofa or under a window, a 2 × 4 holds about 500 LPs and gives you a second display top.
  • Tighter packing: each drawer takes about 90 LPs packed tight, about 10 more across the pair, at the cost of an easy flip.
  • More singles: swap the inner-sleeve stack in each drawer for another row of singles (about 40 more per drawer), and keep spare sleeves in the hidden drawer.
  • LP browser depth: keep it face-out at 24 cm deep. A deeper rack under the turntable would weigh about 35 kg over the sub bay, more than the worktop should carry there.

Paint: a softer green drench

Paint everything in the room one soft grey-green: walls, ceiling, skirting, door and frame, radiator, the alcove, the PC trunking and the unit's face frame. It keeps the colour drench, so the media wall still reads as part of the room, but it is lighter and calmer than the deep forest green, by day especially. The oak, the records' colours and the warm LEDs do the contrast work.

  • Choosing: paint A4 boards of the first three and move them around the room, by day and with the lamps on. Card Room Green is the render's colour. If budget matters, once you've picked one, a trade counter can usually colour-match it into a cheaper range.
  • Finishes: matt or estate emulsion on walls, ceiling and alcove; eggshell in the same colour on skirting, door, trunking and the face frame, which wipes clean and gives the trim a slight sheen.
  • Ceiling: the same colour keeps the drench seamless. A softer green makes the room feel less low than the deep one did, so a separate ceiling colour is less needed.
  • For the OLED: a mid-tone matt wall bounces a little more light onto the screen than the deep green would, but these greens are all still mid-tone (LRV around 20–27). Keep the room's lamps behind or beside the sofa rather than facing the screen, and use the bias light behind the TV.
  • With the oak: grey-greens suit natural and lightly limed oak. Against a softer wall the oak reads less orange than against the deep green.
  • Light: layer warm light: a 2700K floor or table lamp by the sofa alongside the LED strips in the unit.
  • Order: paint before building anything, alcove included, so nothing needs cutting in around the frames, shelves or slats.
  • How much: measure walls and ceiling. A mid green over pale walls usually covers in two coats; over the old colour a mist coat or primer helps.
