The oak joinery

Above the worktop the wall is furniture rather than cladding: two solid oak uprights, shelves carried on joints you can see, and the Sage Green wall left to show between them. Slatted panels are the look of the moment and will date with it; joinery like this won't.

  • Two oak uprights frame the TV: solid oak, 4.5 × 30 cm and about 144 cm long, from the worktop (76) to the ceiling, about 2 cm clear of each side of the screen. They stand on the worktop on two dowels each, are scribed to the ceiling and held to the solid back wall with concealed brackets.
  • Wedged through-tenons: the left shelf (top 156) and the turntable shelf (top 113) pass through the uprights on two tenons each and show 2 cm on the TV side, held with oak wedges. They are the joints you see first, so they're the ones worth cutting by hand.
  • Housed and pegged: the high shelf, in three lengths at 192, and the record ledge are housed into the uprights and pinned with 10 mm oak pegs. The pegs show as dark dots on the uprights' faces.
  • Record ledge above the TV: 7 cm deep with a 1.5 cm lip, top at 155.4, holding four LPs face-out against the wall up to about 186.5, just under the high shelf. The rotating "now playing" wall.
  • LED grooves: a routed groove in each upright's inner face holds a 2700K strip that washes the green wall either side of the TV.
  • Cane screen for the PC: an oak frame with a woven cane panel instead of slats; it hides the ports and lets the exhaust breathe.
  • Solid oak where you touch it: a 3 cm solid oak worktop with a chamfered edge (top at about 76), the face frame in oak rather than painted, so the base reads as one sideboard, and drawer fronts with hand-cut finger pulls.
  • The green wall stays visible behind the TV and between the shelves, so the oak reads as furniture against the colour, not a covering.
  • Notch for the ceiling strip: a 2 × 1.5 cm notch in the back top corner of each upright lets the existing LED strip run straight past.
  • Finish: hardwax oil throughout. A lightly limed or smoked oak for the uprights keeps away from the bright orange "new oak" look.
  • Hidden cables: a 2 × 1.5 cm groove routed down the back of the right-hand upright carries Deco B's 12 V lead and its patch lead to Switch 2, from the high shelf to the cable slot. The TV's leads drop straight into the slot.

Alternative: the slat wall

Kept for reference. The base, drawers, records and consoles are identical; above the worktop, oak acoustic slat panels on battens run from the worktop to the ceiling instead of the uprights, the shelves sit on hidden cleats, and the face frame is painted. It's quicker to fit, hides cables in the batten gap and softens sound, but it follows a current trend.

  • What changes: four 240 × 60 cm slat panels on 25 × 50 mm battens (about £230–380), a laminate worktop instead of solid oak, an MDF face frame painted Sage Green eggshell, and the shelves on cleats with no joints on show.
  • What stays: every height, the record trays, the power and network routes and the lighting, except that the LED wash comes from strips under each shelf instead of the upright grooves.
